## [12.1.1] - 2026-11-18

### Changed
- Import `scipy.interpolate`, `scipy.special` and `scipy.constants` on demand, inside the functions which use them (#1297), i.e. `import felupe` imports only `scipy.sparse` and `scipy.sparse.linalg` of SciPy. Before, these modules (and `scipy.optimize`, `scipy.spatial` and `scipy.fft`, which are imported by `scipy.interpolate`) were about two thirds of the import time of FElupe, e.g. 0.18 s instead of 0.05 s after `import numpy, scipy.sparse.linalg` (CPython 3.13), and in Pyodide they also loaded their WebAssembly modules. `griddata()` is imported by `mesh.fill_between()`, `interp1d()` by `tools.curve()`, `erf()` by `OgdenRoxburgh` and the Stefan-Boltzmann constant by `SolidBodySurfaceRadiation`. The Lagrange polynomials of `ArbitraryOrderLagrangeElement` use `math.factorial()` instead of `scipy.special.factorial()` (the values are identical up to order 24, above, `math.factorial()` is exact). A test checks that `import felupe` doesn't import these modules.

### Fixed
- Fix the precision of a region after a reload, e.g. by `field.merge()` (#1293). `Region.astype(dtype)` stores the data type in the new attribute `Region.dtype` (default is None, i.e. the arrays are not casted) and `Region.reload()` casts the re-evaluated arrays to this data type, i.e. a region keeps its precision. Before, `Region.reload()` evaluated the arrays in double precision again, i.e. `field.merge()` (which reloads the regions of the first fields with the merged meshes), `Region.copy()` and `mesh.update(callback=region.reload)` promoted a single-precision region to `float64`, while the fields kept `float32`, and the items assembled in double precision. The arrays are still evaluated from the undeformed coordinates in double precision and rounded once, also the quantities of the cells of a `RegionBoundary`, which are evaluated by the private method `RegionBoundary._evaluate()` now (before, by `RegionBoundary.reload()`). A copy by `Region.astype(dtype)` is reloaded with the given data type, i.e. a single-precision region is casted back to double precision without a loss of precision.
- Fix the default absolute tolerance of `newtonraphson(..., atol=None)` (#1294), which is an estimate of the round-off errors of the objective function `atol = ||(ε_x sqrt((K ∘ K) (x ∘ x)) + ε_K |K (X - X̄)|)_1||` now. Before, it was `10 ||(ε_x |K| |x| + ε_K |K (X - X̄)|)_1||`, i.e. about 30 to 170 times the round-off errors of the objective function at the converged solution, e.g. of single-precision models of nearly incompressible rubber and steel. Hence, a single-precision model stopped before it converged, e.g. the forces of a uniaxial material test (u-p-J, bulk modulus 5000, `tol=1e-6`, five substeps) had a relative error of `1e-3` instead of `4e-7` (with one more iteration per substep). Now, the estimate is about 3 to 13 times the round-off errors: the round-off errors of the unknowns are random, i.e. the root-sum-square of the products of the Jacobian and the unknowns of each row replaces the sums of their absolute values, and the safety factor is 1 (before, 10). The squares of the Jacobian are evaluated in double precision, i.e. they don't overflow in single precision. The machine epsilons are the ones of the lowest instead of the common precision of the Jacobian and of the objective function, e.g. for `LinearElasticPlasticIsotropicHardening`, which returns its Jacobian in double precision for a single-precision model. Before, the round-off errors of the deformation gradient of such a model were estimated in double precision, i.e. the default absolute tolerance was too small and the model didn't converge for a small tolerance, e.g. `tol=1.49e-8`.
