## [12.1.0] - 2026-11-16

### Added
- Add the Seth-Hill framework `MaterialStrain(..., framework="seth-hill", strain_exponent=m)` for the generalized strain tensors of the Seth-Hill family `E = (C^(m/2) - 1) / m` (`E = ln(C) / 2` for `m=0`), e.g. `strain_exponent=0` for the Hencky strain, `1` for the Biot strain and `2` for the Green strain, in the notation of Miehe and Lambrecht (2001). The stress `T` and the elasticity tensor `𝔼` of the material are dual to the Seth-Hill strain tensor. They are converted to the second Piola-Kirchhoff stress `S = T : ℙ` and to the elasticity tensor `ℂ = ℙ : 𝔼 : ℙ + T : 𝕃` of the Total-Lagrange framework by the projection tensors `ℙ = 2 ∂E/∂C` and `𝕃 = 4 ∂²E/∂C∂C`, which are evaluated by the eigenvalues and the eigenvalue bases of the right Cauchy-Green deformation tensor (Algorithm A of Miehe and Lambrecht). For (nearly) equal eigenvalues, e.g. in the undeformed state, the coefficients `ϑ_ab`, `ξ_ab` and `η` of the projection tensors are evaluated by Taylor series (if the relative difference of the eigenvalues is below `1e-3`) instead of the case distinction of their limits, which avoids the cancellation of the difference quotients. The elasticity tensor is the exact linearization of the first Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor, also for equal eigenvalues. E.g., the linear-elastic material `linear_elastic()` with `strain_exponent=0` is the Hencky material and the linear-elastic-plastic material `linear_elastic_plastic_isotropic_hardening()` is evaluated in the logarithmic strain space (with an additive decomposition of the logarithmic strain tensor). The argument `strain_exponent=None` is required for the Seth-Hill framework and it is not allowed for the other frameworks (`ValueError`). The attribute `MaterialStrain.strain_exponent` is `2` for the Total-Lagrange and `1` for the co-rotational framework. The co-rotational framework is evaluated by the Seth-Hill framework with `m=1` (the results are unchanged up to round-off), by the eigenvalue decomposition of the right Cauchy-Green deformation tensor instead of the singular value decomposition of the deformation gradient.
- Add the argument `atol=None` to `newtonraphson(..., atol=None)` (also for `Job.evaluate(atol=...)`), the absolute tolerance of the norm of the objective function on the active degrees of freedom, in the units of the objective function. If None, it is a bound of the round-off errors of the objective function, `atol = 10 ||(ε_x |K| |x| + ε_K |K (X - X̄)|)_1||`, which is evaluated in each iteration for the updated unknowns `x` with the Jacobian `K` of the iteration and the coordinates `X` of the points of the meshes relative to their centroids `X̄` (only for the fields with as many components as the dimension of their meshes, e.g. displacements). The machine epsilon `ε_x` is the one of the lowest precision of the linear equation system and of the unknowns and `ε_K` is the one of the linear equation system, in which the deformation gradient is evaluated (e.g. double-precision for fields in single-precision on a region in double-precision). The first product estimates the round-off errors of the gradients of the unknowns, e.g. of a rigid body motion, and the second one the round-off errors of the deformation gradient `F = 1 + ∂u/∂X` (with an absolute precision of `ε_K 1`), e.g. in a force-free state. Each row of the products has the unit of the objective function, also for mixed field formulations, i.e. the absolute tolerance is invariant to the units of the model. The relative coordinates don't depend on the location of the meshes, also for items which resist a rigid body translation, e.g. a `CartesianConstraint` (with the absolute coordinates, the absolute tolerance of a cube with a `CartesianConstraint` would increase from `1e-11` to `2e-2` if the mesh is moved by `1e9`). For batched Jacobians of local problems at the quadrature points (solved by `math.solve_2d()`), it is the upper bound `10 ε_x ||K|| ||x||`. The absolute tolerance of the current iteration is available by `IterationState.atol`. Add the argument `return_reference=False` to `tools.fun(items, x, parallel=False, return_reference=False)`, which returns the objective function and the reference norm of the items `max_i ||f - f_i||`, i.e. the largest norm of the objective function without one of the items (the norm of the forces which the other items exert on this item, zero for a single item).
- Add the version of FElupe as a comment at the top of the HTML page of the plotting backend `"webgl"`, e.g. `<!-- Created by FElupe 12.1.0 -->` (right after the doctype), i.e. the version which created a page of `WebGLPlotter.export_html()` or `WebGLPlotter.show()` is traceable. The version is not shown in the plot.
- Add the automatic selection of the plotting backend by `set_plotting_backend(None)`, i.e. `"pyvista"` if PyVista is installed, otherwise `"webgl"`, which requires no optional dependency (only `WebGLPlotter.widget()` requires anywidget). PyVista is only looked up, not imported. The plotting backend `"matplotlib"` is never selected automatically, only by its name. The selected name is returned by `get_plotting_backend()`. The selection is also available by `view.auto_plotting_backend()`, which returns the name without changing the current plotting backend. 
- Add the method `ViewMesh.clip(normal="x", origin=None, invert=True, value=0.0, crinkle=True)` to clip a scene by a plane, also for `ViewField` and `ViewSolid`, e.g. `field.view().clip("-y", origin=(0, 0.5, 0)).plot()`. The arguments are those of `pyvista.DataSetFilters.clip()`: the normal vector of the plane is given by `"x"`, `"y"`, `"z"`, `"-x"`, `"-y"`, `"-z"` or by a (normalized) vector, the origin is a point on the plane (default is the center of the bounds of the points) and the plane is shifted by the value along the normal vector. With `invert=True`, the part of the scene behind the plane is kept, otherwise the part in front of the plane. By default, the clip is crinkled, i.e. all cells with at least one point on the kept side of the plane are kept as a whole, together with their point- and cell-data, and unused points are removed. The clipped scene is of the same class as the scene, i.e. a crinkled clip is evaluated by NumPy and it is supported by all plotting backends. The scene is clipped in the undeformed configuration. Cells which only touch the plane, e.g. by a face, are not kept on both sides of the plane (PyVista keeps them for `invert=False`). With `crinkle=False`, the cells are cut by `pyvista.DataSetFilters.clip()` and a `Scene` with the clipped mesh is returned, which is only supported by the plotting backend `"pyvista"`. The example of the rubber-metal bushing uses the crinkled clip instead of `plotter.mesh.clip(...)` of PyVista.

Before, these items promoted a single-precision model to double precision.

Before, a single-precision model got `float64` vectors and matrices, i.e. the linear equation system and its factorization were promoted to double precision. The items with a boundary region (#1277) and the remaining items of #1272 still assemble in double precision.
- Select the default plotting backend automatically on import of FElupe, i.e. `"pyvista"` if PyVista is installed (as before), otherwise `"webgl"`. Hence, without PyVista, the plot-methods, e.g. `mesh.plot()`, return a `WebGLPlotter` (before, an `ImportError` was raised) and the methods which require PyVista, e.g. `mesh.screenshot()` or the plots of finite elements and regions, raise a `NotImplementedError` (before, an `ImportError`). The automatic selection is restored by `set_plotting_backend(None)`, e.g. after PyVista was installed in a running notebook.
- Evaluate the conversion of the elasticity tensor of the Total-Lagrange framework of `MaterialStrain(..., framework="total-lagrange")` by `numpy.einsum(..., optimize=True)` and add the geometric part directly (before, by `numpy.sum(numpy.broadcast_arrays(...))`), e.g. 84 ms instead of 116 ms for the elasticity tensor of `linear_elastic()` at 64,000 points.
- Change the first Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor of the co-rotational framework of `MaterialStrain(..., framework="co-rotational")` to `P = F S`, where the second Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor `S` is the solution of the Lyapunov equation `U S + S U = 2 T` for the stress tensor `T` of the material. Hence, `T` is the Biot stress tensor, which is work-conjugate to the Biot strain tensor `U - 1`, i.e. `P : δF = T : δU`, and the Cauchy stress tensor is symmetric. Before, it was `P = R T`, which is work-conjugate to the Biot strain tensor only if the stress tensor of the material is coaxial with the stretch tensor, e.g. for isotropic elastic materials like `linear_elastic()`, whose results are unchanged (up to round-off). Otherwise, e.g. for elastic-plastic or viscoelastic materials under non-proportional loading or for anisotropic materials, the Cauchy stress tensor was not symmetric, i.e. the balance of angular momentum was violated, and the elasticity tensor was not symmetric. The difference of the stresses is of the order of the stress times the strain. The solution of the Lyapunov equation `S_ab = 2 T_ab / (λ_a + λ_b)` (with the principal stretches `λ_a`) is evaluated in the principal axes of the stretch tensor. The elasticity tensor is the exact linearization of `P = F S`, which is symmetric for a symmetric elasticity tensor of the material, e.g. for `linear_elastic_plastic_isotropic_hardening()`. The strain and stress tensors of the frameworks are listed in the how-to guide *Define Strain-Based Materials*.
- Change the convergence criterion of `newtonraphson()` to `||f_1|| < atol + tol f_ref` with the reference norm `f_ref = max(||f_0||, max_i ||f - f_i||)`, i.e. the larger of the norm of the reaction forces and the largest norm of the forces between the items, e.g. of a `ContactRigidPlane` or of a `SolidBodyPressure` on a `SolidBody`. The relative norms of the objective function `fnorm = ||f_1|| / (f_ref + atol / tol)` are compared with the (relative) tolerance `tol`, as before. Before, the criterion was `||f_1|| / (1e-3 + ||f_0||) < tol`, i.e. with a hard-coded absolute tolerance of `1e-3 tol` (e.g. `1.5e-11` for double-precision) in the units of the objective function and only relative to the reaction forces. Hence, models with large forces which are not reacted on the prescribed degrees of freedom did not converge, e.g. a rigid body motion of a stiff solid body before it gets in contact with a `ContactRigidPlane` (steel in mm/MPa or m/Pa, the round-off errors of the forces of about `5e-10` exceeded the absolute tolerance; now one iteration per substep) or a solid body with a self-equilibrated pressure on all faces and statically determinate supports. Models with small forces converged too early, e.g. a uniaxial tension with a shear modulus of `1e-9` converged in one iteration with a residual of 7.5% of the reaction force (now in four iterations, like with a shear modulus of one). For a single item with reaction forces, the convergence is unchanged and the relative norms of the objective function are slightly larger (without the `1e-3` in the denominator). The private function `tools._newton.check()` takes the arguments `atol=0.0` and `reference=0.0` instead of `eps=1e-3`, which are passed by `newtonraphson()` to a custom `check`. The check of the trials of a plugin, e.g. of a `LinesearchPlugin`, uses the same absolute tolerance and the same reference norm. A function without a reference norm (no prescribed degrees of freedom and no items) is checked by the absolute tolerance only, e.g. a double root requires a given absolute tolerance.
- Update the highlights in README.md and in the documentation: a pure-Python package with NumPy and SciPy as the only required dependencies (before: *100% Python*, the package also contains the JavaScript of the WebGL viewer and of the dashboard), the job plugins (new) and the plots with the plotting backends `"pyvista"`, `"webgl"` and `"matplotlib"` (before: only PyVista), with links to the how-to guides *Use Plugins* and *Choose a Plotting Backend*.
- Document the two meanings of `points` in FElupe. For a `Mesh`, `points` are the point coordinates. For an object that refers to a mesh, for example a `Boundary`, `points` are the point ids. The docstrings of `Boundary` and `Mesh`, the tutorial *Building Blocks* and the how-to guide *Generate Meshes* include this information.
- Add docstrings with examples to `tools.force()`, `tools.moment()` and `tools.curve()`. Add `tools.curve()` to the API reference of the tools.

### Fixed
- Assemble the remaining items in the precision of the fields: the gap vectors of the contact items `ContactRigidPlane` and `ContactRigidCylinder` (the sum of the gap vectors of the undeformed configuration and the relative displacements) are casted to the precision of the field values, the area, the undeformed length, the kinematics and the material results of a `TrussBody` use this precision, `OgdenRoxburgh` returns its stress and tangent in the precision of the base material, and `FreeVibration` allocates its stiffness and mass matrix in the precision of the unknowns (the identity of `mass_matrix()` uses the precision of the field values, too). Before, these items assembled `float64` vectors and matrices for single-precision fields, see #1272.
- Evaluate the values of a linear or bilinear form of the `Form` API (`form.integrate()`, `form.assemble()` and `FormItem`) in the precision of the fields: the values of `LinearForm.integrate()` and `BilinearForm.integrate()` are allocated with the common data type of the field values of the test and the trial field and of the differential volumes `dx` (before, `numpy.zeros()` and hence `float64`, i.e. every user-defined weak form assembled in double precision for single-precision fields).
- Fix the precision of a `RegionBoundary` and of its items: `RegionBoundary.astype(dtype)` casts the area vectors `dA`, the unit normal vectors `normals` and the unit tangent vectors `tangents` now (before, only `dV` was casted by `Region.astype()`), i.e. the quantities are evaluated from the undeformed coordinates in double precision and rounded once, like the gradients of the shape functions. The weights of the quadrature are not casted. `SolidBodyCauchyStress` holds its prescribed Cauchy stress tensor in the precision of the field values, also after `update()`. Hence, `SolidBodyPressure` and `SolidBodyCauchyStress` assemble in the precision of the fields on a casted boundary region (before, they assembled `float64` vectors and matrices for single-precision fields).
- Fix the area measure `dV` of a `RegionBoundary` after `copy()`, `reload()` and `astype()`. The area vectors `dA`, the area measures `dV`, the unit normal vectors `normals` and the unit tangent vectors `tangents` are evaluated in `RegionBoundary._init_faces()` from the undeformed coordinates, but `Region.reload()` (which is called by `Region.copy()` and `Region.astype()`) replaced `dV` by the volumetric expression `det(dXdr) * weights` of the cells on the boundary, without a re-evaluation of the boundary quantities. Hence, `dV` was reduced by a factor of `2 (n - 1)` (`n`: points per edge of the mesh), while `dA` and `normals` were kept, i.e. `dA / dV` was not equal to the unit normal vectors and the loads of the items which integrate over a boundary region, e.g. `SolidBodyPressure` and `SolidBodyCauchyStress`, were reduced by this factor: a pressure of one on the face of a cube with 8 hexahedrons gave a total force of 0.25 instead of 1 after a single `copy()`, `reload()` or `astype()`. `RegionBoundary.reload()` re-evaluates `_init_faces()` now, and `_init_faces()` no longer modifies `dXdr` inplace (the normal vector of an edge was evaluated by a modification of a view of `dXdr`), i.e. the boundary quantities are always consistent with the current `dXdr` and quadrature. The boundary quantities of a fresh region are unchanged.
- Fix the state variables of a composite material `CompositeMaterial(material, other_material)`, i.e. `material & other_material`. Before, only the state variables of the first material were considered: both materials were evaluated with the state variables of the first material and only its updated state variables were returned. Hence, the order of the materials mattered, e.g. `NeoHooke(mu=1) & viscoelastic` raised a broadcasting error inside the viscoelastic material (the solid body allocated state variables of shape `(0, ...)`), while `viscoelastic & NeoHooke(mu=1)` worked, and two materials with state variables gave wrong results without an error, e.g. two viscoelastic branches with six state variables each (the second branch was evaluated with the state variables of the first branch and its own state variables were never updated). Now, each material keeps its own state variables: the state variables of the composite material are the flattened state variables of the first material, followed by the flattened state variables of the second material, i.e. `CompositeMaterial.x[-1]` has the size of both state variables (before, it was taken from the first material). The state variables are split for the evaluation of the gradients and the hessians of the materials and the updated state variables are concatenated again. This also works for nested composite materials, e.g. `a & b & c`, and for state variables with more than one axis, e.g. `self.x = [np.eye(3), np.zeros((3, 3))]`. State variables of a wrong size raise a `ValueError`. If the state variables are not given (None) or if the input list contains only one array, the input list is passed to both materials and the state variables of the first material are returned (as before).
- Fix the fourth-order elasticity tensor of the co-rotational framework of `MaterialStrain(..., framework="co-rotational")`, which is the exact linearization of the first Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor now, i.e. Newton's method converges quadratically. Before, it was an approximation (with a warning in the docstring): the elasticity tensor of the material was rotated by `R_iI R_kK C_IJKL` and the geometric part `δ_ik T_JL` of the Total-Lagrange framework was added, which is the derivative of `δF T`, not of `δR T`. Its relative error was of the order of the strains, e.g. 13% to 25% at principal stretches of 0.68 to 1.18, also without a rotation. E.g., a cube which was rotated by 90° and stretched by 30% took 210 instead of 48 iterations (linear-elastic, 12 substeps), with an increasing number of iterations per substep, and the elastic-plastic material with isotropic hardening did not converge with four substeps (now 23 iterations). Now, the elasticity tensor is evaluated by the components of the stress and of the elasticity tensor of the material in the principal axes of the stretch tensor. The evaluation of the elasticity tensor is not slower. `MaterialStrain.extract()` returns the rotation tensor (as before).
- Fix the temporary pages of the plotting backend `"webgl"`, i.e. of `WebGLPlotter.show()` without a filename outside of a notebook, on systems with more than one user (POSIX). Before, the pages were written to the folder `felupe` of the temporary directory, which is shared by all users, e.g. `/tmp`. Hence, the user who showed the first page owned the folder and the pages of all other users failed with a `PermissionError`. A folder which was created in advance by another user and which was writable by others allowed this user to exchange a page by a symbolic link, after the empty file was created and before the page was written to it (by its path), i.e. the page overwrote a file of the user who showed the plot (also with `fs.protected_symlinks`, which only protects sticky folders), or to exchange a page before the web browser loaded it. Now, the pages are written to the private folder `felupe-<uid>` with the id of the user (`felupe` on Windows, where the temporary directory is a folder of the user), which is created with the permissions `0o700`. A folder which is not a directory of the user (e.g. a symbolic link) or which is writable by the group or by others is not used, then the pages of the process are written to a new private folder `felupe-<uid>-<8 characters>` by `tempfile.mkdtemp()` with a warning (once per process). The private folders of earlier processes which are older than a day are removed together with their pages by the next new private folder (folders which are not private and folders with other files are kept). The temporary file is created exclusively and the page is written by the handle of the file, i.e. an existing file or a symbolic link is never followed. A page which can not be written is removed, e.g. if the disk is full.
- Fix the atomic writes of the live HTML file of `DashboardWidgetPlugin(filename=...)`. Before, the temporary file had the fixed name of the file with the suffix `.tmp`, e.g. `job.state.js.tmp`, which followed an existing symbolic link of this name, e.g. in a folder which is writable by other users (without a sticky bit), and which was shared by the processes which wrote to the same file. Now, the temporary file has a random name, it is created exclusively (with the permissions of the umask, as before) and it is removed if the file is not written, also on an error or an interrupt, e.g. if the disk is full or by a `KeyboardInterrupt`.
- Fix the HTML pages of the WebGL viewer and of the dashboard with a label or a title which contains a placeholder of the page, e.g. `/*MODULE*/`. Before, the placeholders were replaced one after another, i.e. a placeholder in the inline JSON of the scene (or in the inline config of the dashboard, which is JavaScript) was replaced by the source code of the module, and the page did not load. Now, all placeholders are replaced in one pass by the internal function `view._backends._webgl._page.fill(template, values)`.

## [12.0.0] - 2026-10-30

### Added
- Add `DashboardWidgetPlugin(title=None, interval=0.1)`, a job plugin with a compact live dashboard of a job in a Jupyter widget, e.g. in JupyterLab, Jupyter Notebook or the interactive window of VS Code. It shows the model of the latest converged substep with banded contours of the norm of the first field of the unknowns (e.g. displacements or temperature), deformed by this field if it has as many components as the points, the load path with one cell per substep including the load factors of the increments of a `CutbackPlugin`, the solver figures (residual norm, tolerance, the residual norms of the attempts of the current substep, iterations, cutbacks, failed attempts), the properties of the model (points, cells and cell type of the mesh, finite element and quadrature of the region, degrees of freedom) and performance statistics (assembly, linear solve, residual and update, time per iteration, peak memory). The model is drawn from the cells of the regions of all items (without boundary regions), e.g. of several solid bodies on the meshes of a `MeshContainer`, and the values are taken from the first field of the unknowns `x0` of the job (the other fields may be defined on other meshes). The widget is updated while the cell is running and all controls (scale, mesh lines, view) work in the frontend. The default view of a 3d-model is the default view of the plots of FElupe, e.g. of `SolidBody.plot()`, with the z-axis upwards. Only the field values of the latest converged substep are kept. Only the points of the outer surface and their values are transferred to the frontend, the limits of the legend are evaluated by the values at all points. It requires the new optional dependency `anywidget` (`pip install felupe[dashboard]`, also included in `felupe[all]`). Without a Jupyter frontend, e.g. in a script in a terminal or in the IPython terminal, a warning is raised once and the dashboard is not displayed (the state of the job is collected anyway). A static snapshot of the dashboard after the job, `DashboardWidgetPlugin.snapshot()`, is a self-contained HTML page, which is shown in the output of a cell (in an iframe, whose height and theme follow the dashboard and the parent page), e.g. in the examples of the documentation which are executed by sphinx-gallery without a Jupyter kernel, or saved as a HTML file by `snapshot.save("job.html")`. With `DashboardWidgetPlugin(filename="job.html", file_interval=2.0)`, a live HTML file of the dashboard is written (also without a Jupyter frontend and without anywidget), which is opened in the web browser without a Jupyter frontend. The state is written to a script next to the page at most every `file_interval` seconds (only if it has changed, each file is replaced atomically) and the page polls the scripts and updates itself without a reload (no server is required).
- Enhance `SolidBodyNearlyIncompressible` with the new argument `SolidBodyNearlyIncompressible(..., volumetric="volume-ratio")` for the volumetric part of the strain energy density function, given by a kind with the bulk modulus or by an instance of `VolumetricStrainEnergy` (then, without the bulk modulus). The built-in kinds are quadratic in the Seth-Hill strain `E_m = (λ^m - 1) / m` of the volumetric stretch `λ = J^(1/3)`, i.e. `U(J) = 9K/2 E_m^2`: `"volume-ratio"` for `U(J) = K (J - 1)^2 / 2` (m=3, default, as before), `"stretch"` for `U(J) = 9K (J^(1/3) - 1)^2 / 2` (m=1) and `"log"` for `U(J) = K ln(J)^2 / 2` (limit m → 0). The argument `bulk=None` is optional now and it is only required for the kinds. A missing bulk modulus, an unknown kind or a bulk modulus together with an instance raise a `ValueError`.
- Add `VolumetricStrainEnergy(bulk, kind="volume-ratio")`, the volumetric part of the strain energy density function as a function of the volume ratio. An instance `U` is callable, i.e. `U(J)`, `U.dUdJ(J)` and `U.d2UdJdJ(J)` return the function and its first and second derivatives, for the kinds `"volume-ratio"`, `"stretch"` and `"log"`. Unlike a `ConstitutiveMaterial`, it is evaluated by the volume ratio `J`, not by the deformation gradient. A custom volumetric part is a subclass which implements `__call__(J)`, `dUdJ(J)` and `d2UdJdJ(J)`. It is used by `NeoHooke`, `Volumetric`, `NearlyIncompressible` and `SolidBodyNearlyIncompressible`, which provide it by the attribute `volumetric`.
- Add the argument `volumetric="volume-ratio"` to `NeoHooke(mu=None, bulk=None, parallel=False, volumetric="volume-ratio")` and `Volumetric(bulk=None, parallel=False, volumetric="volume-ratio")` for the volumetric part of the strain energy density function, given by a kind with the bulk modulus or by an instance of `VolumetricStrainEnergy` (then, without the bulk modulus), e.g. `NeoHooke(mu=1, bulk=5000, volumetric="stretch")`. The default results are bit-identical. Without the bulk modulus, `NeoHooke` has no volumetric part (as before). The argument `bulk` of `Volumetric` is optional now (it is required for the kinds).
- Add `solve.SuperLUSolver(permc_spec="MMD_AT_PLUS_A", diag_pivot_thresh=0.0, diag_perturbation=None, symmetric_mode=True, relax=2, panel_size=20, options=None, refine=True, tol=None, fallback_pivot_thresh=0.1)`, a sparse direct solver by `scipy.sparse.linalg.splu()` with options tuned for the (structurally) symmetric matrices of the finite element method. An instance is a solver function `x = solver(A, b)`, e.g. `Job.evaluate(solver=fem.solve.SuperLUSolver())`. The symmetric mode of SuperLU with the minimum degree ordering of the structure of `A^T + A` requires only a symmetric structure of the matrix, i.e. it is also used for non-symmetric values, e.g. of follower loads or of frictional contacts, and no check of the symmetry is required. By default, the diagonal entries are taken as pivots (no pivoting), i.e. the fill-reducing ordering of the columns is kept. The large off-diagonal entries of penalty-based items, e.g. of a `MultiPointConstraint` or a `ContactRigidPlane`, do not lead to pivoting, which would increase the fill-in and the time of the factorization by orders of magnitude, e.g. a `ThreeFieldVariation` of a mesh with hexahedrons and 14,293 degrees of freedom is factorized in 0.8 s instead of 115 s with a pivot threshold of 0.1. However, SuperLU never takes a diagonal entry of zero as pivot. The zero diagonal entries of saddle point problems, e.g. of the pressure fields of `ThreeFieldVariation` and `NearlyIncompressible` (on `FieldsMixed`) or of a mixed weak form by `Form`, lead to an exchange of rows if they are eliminated before they are filled by the elimination of one of their neighbours. Hence, the zero diagonal entries `A_ii = 0` are perturbed before the factorization (static pivoting, like PARDISO) by `-ε Σ_j A_ji² / |A_jj|` (sum over the rows `j` with non-zero diagonal entries), i.e. by the relative perturbation `ε` (the argument `diag_perturbation`, if None, the square root of the machine epsilon of the precision of the matrix) of an estimate of their pivots (the diagonal entries of the Schur complement with the inverse of the diagonal of the other block). The perturbation is invariant to the scaling of the unknowns, e.g. by the units of the displacements and the pressure, and its sign is the sign of the pivots of a symmetric saddle point problem with a positive definite block. The matrix with the perturbed zero diagonal entries is returned by `SuperLUSolver.perturb(A)`. The solution of the perturbed matrix is improved by iterative refinement with the original matrix until the relative correction (maximum norms) is below the tolerance (at most ten steps, stopped if the correction is not reduced by at least a factor of two), usually by one or two steps. If the iterative refinement does not converge or if the backward error exceeds the tolerance, the original matrix is factorized again with the pivot threshold `fallback_pivot_thresh`, e.g. for an exactly singular matrix. With `diag_perturbation=0` or `refine=False`, the zero diagonal entries are not perturbed. E.g., an incompressible mixed u/p-formulation by `Form` with a constant pressure per cell on a mesh with 512 quadratic hexahedrons and 7,215 degrees of freedom is solved in 1.5 s instead of 20 s without perturbation (with 2,221 exchanges of rows, the fill-in of the factors is increased by a factor of about seven). Without pivoting, the factorization is not guaranteed to be stable. Hence, the normwise backward error of the solution `||b - A x|| / (||A|| ||x|| + ||b||)` (maximum norms) is evaluated by `SuperLUSolver.backward_error(A, x, b, r=None)`, which requires one sparse matrix-vector product and which does not depend on the condition number of the matrix. If the backward error exceeds the tolerance `tol` (if None, the square root of the machine epsilon of the lowest precision of the matrix and the right-hand side) or if the first factorization fails, the matrix is factorized again with the pivot threshold `fallback_pivot_thresh`. With `fallback_pivot_thresh=None`, the matrix is factorized only once and the backward error is not evaluated (except for the iterative refinement of a perturbed matrix). Before the evaluation of the backward error, the solution is improved by one step of iterative refinement (`refine=True`), which re-uses the factors of the matrix. The evaluation of the backward error and the refinement take less than 1% of the time of the factorization for models with more than a few thousand degrees of freedom. Compared to `scipy.sparse.linalg.spsolve()`, the factorization is faster, e.g. by a factor of about two for meshes with hexahedrons and of about 1.5 for meshes with quads, and the fill-in of the factors is lower. Small relaxed supernodes (`relax=2`, the default of SuperLU is 10) are as fast as larger ones for meshes with linear cells and in 2d, but faster for meshes with quadratic cells and for mixed field formulations in 3d, e.g. by a factor of up to four compared to `relax=20`. The size of the panels (`panel_size=20`, the default of SuperLU) has no significant influence. Like `spsolve()`, a single right-hand side returns a 1d-array and an exactly singular matrix emits a `MatrixRankWarning` and returns NaN values. Multiple right-hand sides and sparse right-hand sides are supported. The LU decomposition is available by `SuperLUSolver.factorize(A, diag_pivot_thresh=None)` (if None, the pivot threshold of the solver).

The values of an additional rotation-point are the rotations (in rad) of the connected points about the center-point, i.e. the points are moved rigidly by the displacements of the center-point and by a rotation, which may be prescribed or free, e.g. for a twisted end face of a beam or for the rigid core of a coated sphere which rolls on a plane. Without a rotation-point, the rotations are locked (unchanged). The rotation matrix is parametrized incrementally, `R = exp([ψ - ψ_n]×) R_n`, with the rotation-vector `ψ_n` and the rotation matrix `R_n` of the last converged substep as state variables. ### Changed
- The state variables of `ContactRigidPlane` (the gap vectors, the reference gap vectors of the frictional contact and the flags of active contact points) are now only updated after a converged increment, like the state variables of a `SolidBody`. They are stored in `results.statevars` (converged) and `results._statevars` (trial). The forces and the stiffness matrix are functions of the displacements within an increment, evaluated by a return mapping which starts from the converged state. Before, the reference gap vectors were moved in each evaluation of the vector, e.g. also by trial evaluations of a `LinesearchPlugin` and by failed Newton-iterations, and the stiffness matrix depended on the order of the evaluations of the vector and the matrix. The attributes `results.dx_ref`, `results.active` and `results.slip` now hold the trial results of the last evaluation. The checkpoint of a `ContactRigidPlane` contains the state variables and the multipliers.
- The stiffness matrix of `ContactRigidPlane` is now consistent for frictional contact. For sliding points, the derivative of the direction of the friction force is evaluated with the trial (elastic) slip of the converged reference, and the non-symmetric coupling of the friction force to the normal force is included. For points which come into contact within an increment, the reference gap vector is located at the linearly interpolated point of first contact (zero gap), with its derivative in the stiffness matrix. Before, the reference was the gap vector of the first Newton-iteration with an active contact.
- The stiffness-based multipliers of `ContactRigidPlane` are evaluated by the mean stiffness of the diagonal blocks of the stiffness matrices of the items at all points in potential contact (before: the active points). Hence, they are defined for the first increment of a contact and they do not depend on the number of active points. The mean tangential stiffness is divided by the number of tangential directions (one in 2d, before: two). `ContactRigidPlane.contact_multipliers()` takes no arguments.
- Assemble the vector and the matrix of `ContactRigidPlane` from vectorized COO-arrays, instead of setting items of LIL-matrices. Before, the blocks of the points in contact were assembled from dense arrays of shape (n * dim, n * dim), i.e. the time and the memory scaled quadratically with the number of points in contact n (e.g. 31 s for 5000 points). Now, the assembly scales linearly (e.g. 14 ms for 5000 points).
- `newtonraphson()` creates one `IterationState` which is updated in-place during all iterations. Before, a new state was created after each iteration and the hooks `before_newton` and `before_iteration` got an empty state or the state of the previous iteration. The `before_newton` hook is now triggered after the unknowns are determined and before the initial assembly of the objective function.
- The check for NaN values in the Newton increment is now performed before the `after_linear_solve` hook is triggered.
- Copy `StateNearlyIncompressible.u` from `field[0].values`, instead of using it directly.
- `Step.generate()` catches errors of the Newton-Raphson method of a substep and triggers the `after_failed_substep` hook of the plugins. If the substep is not recovered by a plugin, the error is raised (unchanged).
- The unknowns `x0` are now linked to the result of each completed substep by `Step.generate()` (before by `Job.evaluate()`). Hence, also a standalone `Step.generate()` starts each substep from the result of the previous substep.
- The hook `after_substep` gets the `SubstepState` of the substep (a subclass of `JobState` with the same attributes `stepnumber`, `substepnumber` and `time`). The `ProgressPlugin` shows the increments of subdivided substeps in this hook.
- The hook `after_job` is now also triggered if the evaluation of a job fails. The error is available as `JobState.error` and it is raised after all plugins are called.
- `FieldContainer.checkpoint()` shares the regions, meshes, mesh containers and indices of the fields with the field container, only the field containers, the fields and their values are copied. Before, the whole field container was deep-copied, including the regions with the gradients of the shape functions, e.g. 84 MB instead of 0.7 MB for a checkpoint of 8000 hexahedrons. This also applies to `SolidBody.checkpoint()`, `SolidBodyNearlyIncompressible.checkpoint()` and the `CutbackPlugin`, which creates a checkpoint before each substep. Changes of the region or the mesh after a checkpoint is created are visible in the field container of the checkpoint, the restore of the values is not affected. The field container of the checkpoint holds the copied top-level field container `"field.x0"` as `x0` (before, `x0` was copied twice).
- Change the perturbation in `eigh` and `eigvalsh` with new helpers in the JAX backend. The helper is enabled in `becker()`, `storakers()`, `extended_tube()`, `ogden()` and `morph()`.
- Change the rate tensor in `morph()` to a symmetric tensor.
- Evaluate the bilinear form of the gradients of the test and the trial field in `IntegralFormCartesian.integrate(parallel=False)` in chunks of cells with a fixed contraction path. NumPy's default path limits the intermediate arrays to the size of the largest input array and hence, contracts the last three operands at once for many element types. The chunks are sized to fit into the CPU caches (`assembly._cartesian.CHUNKSIZE_BYTES`, 2 MiB, about the L2 cache of one core). This speeds up the integration of the stiffness matrix by about 1.2-2.5x (linear tetrahedrons), 3-5x (linear hexahedrons) and up to 25x for a single Lagrange hexahedron of order 4. The results are equal up to rounding errors.
- Assemble bilinear forms in `IntegralFormCartesian.assemble()` with a cached sparsity pattern of the test and the trial field. The values are summed up by `numpy.bincount()` into the data array of the CSR matrix, instead of converting all (duplicate) values from the COO to the CSR format on each assembly. The pattern is created on the first assembly (from the unique pairs of points per cell) and it is weakly referenced by the indices of the fields, i.e. it is not copied with the fields and it is released together with them. Fields with degrees of freedom which are not numbered point-wise fall back to the COO-assembly. This speeds up the assembly of the stiffness matrix by about 4-5x.
- Evaluate the COO-indices `IntegralFormCartesian.indices` of a bilinear form on demand. These are only required if the form is not assembled with a sparsity pattern.
- Return the assembled sparse matrix (vector) of a single block in `IntegralForm.assemble(block=True)` directly, without the block-assembly by `scipy.sparse.bmat()` (`scipy.sparse.vstack()`), which copied the matrix of a single field.
- Evaluate the linear form of the gradient of the test field in `IntegralFormCartesian.integrate()` by a single contraction (`optimize=False`). This is about 1.1-11x faster than NumPy's default contraction path, e.g. for the internal force vector.
- The update of a field container in `newtonraphson()` shares the regions, meshes and indices of the fields with the field container of the last iteration (like `FieldContainer.checkpoint()`), only the field containers, the fields and their values are copied. Before, the whole field container was deep-copied in each iteration, including the regions with the gradients of the shape functions. The arithmetic operators of a field container, e.g. `x + dx`, are not changed.
- Evaluate the chunks of the bilinear form of the gradients of the test and the trial field in `IntegralFormCartesian.integrate(parallel=True)` by a pool of threads (one per usable CPU of the process, e.g. the CPUs of a job on a shared HPC-node), instead of `einsumt` with NumPy's default contraction path. With a multi-threaded BLAS-library, limit its number of threads to avoid an oversubscription of the CPUs.
- Use the `dtype` from the `field` values for the `statevars` in `SolidBody` and `SolidBodyNearlyIncompressible` and `ContactRigidPlane`.
- Use the `dtype` from the `field` values for `SparsityPattern.assemble()`.

- Assemble the vectors and matrices of `MultiPointConstraint` and `MultiPointContact` from vectorized COO-arrays, instead of setting items of LIL-matrices. Before, the sparse identity matrix of the connected points was converted to a dense array of shape (n, n) in the assembly of the matrix, i.e. the time and the memory scaled quadratically with the number of points n (e.g. 5.5 s for 10k points, 80 GiB for 100k points). Now, the assembly scales linearly, e.g. 3 ms for 10k points and 26 ms for 100k points. The results are unchanged.
- Move the evaluation of `numpy.einsum()` in chunks from `assembly._cartesian` to `math._einsum.einsum_chunks()`, together with `math._einsum.cpu_count()` (both are not part of the public API). The operands are now sliced by the chunked index of their subscripts (before: by the length of their last axis), i.e. operands without this index are not sliced, also if their last axis has the length of the cells. Optionally, the chunks are taken along another free index of the output (`idx`, default is the last axis of the output), the output array is created if it is not given (`out=None`) and the items are divided into one chunk per thread by default (`chunksize=None`).
- Evaluate the contractions of `IntegralFormCartesian.integrate(parallel=True)` (the linear forms and the bilinear forms without the gradients of both fields) and of `assembly.expression.BasisField(parallel=True)` by `math._einsum.einsum_chunks()` in chunks of cells (one per thread), instead of `einsumt`. The chunks are written directly into the output array, without concatenating them. This speeds up the parallel evaluation of the bases by about 2x and avoids the overhead of the threads if there are less cells than threads, e.g. for a single cell of high order. The serial evaluation is unchanged. The assembly does not use `einsumt` anymore.
- `math._einsum.einsum_chunks()` passes a single chunk in serial (`parallel=False, chunksize=None`) to `numpy.einsum()` without further checks, i.e. its result has the memory layout chosen by NumPy. The output array of more chunks is created in C-order, which is faster for the sparse assembly. The output array of the bilinear form of the gradients of both fields in `IntegralFormCartesian.integrate()` is always created in C-order, also for a single chunk.
- Evaluate the contractions of the math helpers `math.dot()`, `math.ddot()`, `math.dddot()`, `math.cdya_ik()`, `math.cdya_il()` and `math.cdya()` and of `constitution.AreaChange` with `parallel=True` by `math._einsum.einsum_chunks()` in chunks of cells (one per thread), instead of `einsumt`. The serial evaluation (`parallel=False`) is unchanged. FElupe does not use `einsumt` anymore.
- `math._einsum.einsum_chunks()` re-uses a pool of threads (one per usable CPU), which is created on its first use. Before, a new pool was created for each evaluation. A forked child process creates its own pool. This reduces the overhead of the threads for small arrays, e.g. the parallel assembly of the vector and the matrix of a mixed u/p/J-formulation with 64 hexahedrons is about 1.3-2x faster. Optional keyword-arguments are passed to `numpy.einsum()` and contractions without a free last index of the output, e.g. with a scalar output, are evaluated as a single chunk (instead of raising an error).
- The methods of a material decorated by `constitutive_material()` take precedence over the methods of `ConstitutiveMaterial`, i.e. the order of the base classes of the derived class is reversed. Hence, a class which is already derived from `ConstitutiveMaterial` may be decorated (before, this raised a `TypeError`).
- Move the pool of threads and `cpu_count()` from `math._einsum` to `math._threads`. Add `math._threads.map_threaded()`, which evaluates calls from a thread of the pool in serial, e.g. a math-function with `parallel=True` within a chunk of a constitutive material. Otherwise, a task of the pool which waits for further tasks of the same pool may block all threads of the pool.
- Evaluate the gradient and the hessian of `NeoHooke` and `NeoHookeCompressible` in chunks of cells, i.e. the materials implement `_gradient()` and `_hessian()` of `ConstitutiveMaterial`. This speeds up the evaluation of the hessian by about 1.4x and the assembly of the stiffness matrix of a `SolidBody` by about 1.2-1.4x (27k hexahedrons). With `parallel=True`, the chunks are evaluated by a pool of threads (before: the math-functions within the material), which speeds up the evaluation of the hessian by about 3x (2 CPUs). The output arrays are handled by `ConstitutiveMaterial`, i.e. the materials don't re-use output arrays on their own. The strain energy density functions are evaluated without threads.
- Evaluate the gradients and the hessians of the composed materials `ThreeFieldVariation`, `NearlyIncompressible`, `ThirdMediumContactMixed`, `OgdenRoxburgh`, `CompositeMaterial` and `MaterialStrain` in chunks of cells, i.e. they implement `_gradient()` and `_hessian()` of `ConstitutiveMaterial` and the (inner) material is evaluated for each chunk. This speeds up the evaluation of the hessians by about 1.2-2x (27k hexahedrons), e.g. `ThreeFieldVariation(NeoHooke())` by about 2x. The composed materials don't pass output arrays to the (inner) material anymore.
- The argument `parallel` of `ThreeFieldVariation`, `NearlyIncompressible` and `ThirdMediumContactMixed` defaults to None, i.e. the flag is taken from the material, e.g. `ThreeFieldVariation(NeoHooke(mu=1, bulk=5000, parallel=True))` evaluates the chunks of cells by a pool of threads. Add the argument `parallel=None` to `OgdenRoxburgh`. A `CompositeMaterial` evaluates the chunks by threads if any of its materials is parallel.
- The intermediate results of `ThreeFieldVariation` are local variables of a chunk (before: attributes of the material) and `MaterialStrain` does not change its keyword-argument `tangent` during the evaluation, i.e. both materials are thread-safe. Remove the unused fourth-order projection tensor in the hessian of `ThreeFieldVariation`.
- Evaluate the gradients and the hessians of the tensortrax-based materials `Hyperelastic` and `MaterialAD` in chunks of cells by `ConstitutiveMaterial`, instead of the chunks of `tensortrax`. This includes the conversions of the stresses and the elasticity tensors, e.g. of the second Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor and its tangent, which speeds up the evaluation of the hessian of `Hyperelastic(neo_hooke)` by about 2x (27k hexahedrons). With `parallel=True`, the chunks are evaluated by the pool of threads of FElupe; `tensortrax` is evaluated without threads. The JAX-based materials are unchanged.

The elasticity tensor is constant and it is evaluated without threads (before: `parallel=True` evaluated the math-functions of the constant elasticity tensor by threads and the stress tensor without threads).

Note that `framework="total-lagrange"` will change the linear-elastic material model formulation to the Saint-Venant Kirchhoff material model formulation.

- Add the Ogden material model formulation also for the JAX-backend `constitution.jax.models.hyperelastic.ogden`.
- Add `linear_elastic_viscoelastic()`, a linear-elastic (deviatoric) viscoelastic material model formulation  to be used in `MaterialStrain`.

### Changed
- Don't enforce the returned elasticity tensor in `MaterialStrain` to be major-symmetric.
- Change the default output of the loadcases `dof.uniaxial()`, `dof.shear()` and `dof.biaxial()` to return the dict of boundary conditions, but deprecate this `boundaries, loadcase = fem.dof.uniaxial(field, return_loadcase=None)` (was `boundaries, loadcase = fem.dof.uniaxial(field)` before). The additional `loadcase` dict is now optional, `boundaries, loadcase = fem.dof.uniaxial(field, return_loadcase=True)` is required.
- Don't support iteration over a `BoundaryDict`. Use `BoundaryDict.items()` or `BoundaryDict.values()` instead.
- Change the default label value in `Boundary.plot(label="Boundary Condition")` (was `label=None` before). The fall-back to `Boundary.name` is removed.

### Deprecated
- Deprecate `bounds` in `dof.symmetry(field, bounds=None)`. Use new equivalent `boundaries` argument, `dof.symmetry(field, boundaries=None)`.
- Deprecate `boundaries, loadcase = fem.dof.uniaxial(field, return_loadcase=None)`. Use `return_loadcase=True` or `return_loadcase=False` in new code. `return_loadcase=False` will be the default value in 11.0.0.

### Removed
- Remove deprecated `SolidBodyGravity`.
- Remove support for Python 3.9.
- Remove unused optional `name` argument in `Boundary`.
